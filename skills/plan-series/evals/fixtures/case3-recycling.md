# Town of Halbrook — Recycling Rules, effective 1 March

Summary prepared by the Public Works department from the new waste ordinance and the hauler contract. It replaces the leaflet of two years ago.

## For residents

Halbrook now has three bins: blue for recycling, green for food and garden waste, black for everything else.

The blue bin takes paper, cardboard, metal cans, and plastic bottles and tubs. Glass no longer goes in the blue bin; it goes to the glass banks at the four supermarkets.

Items go in the blue bin loose. Recycling in a plastic bag is treated as black-bin waste, because the sorting plant cannot open bags.

Containers should be empty and scraped. They do not need to be washed.

Pizza boxes with grease stains go in the green bin, not the blue one.

Soft plastic (film, wrappers, carrier bags) goes in the black bin. Some supermarkets take it back at their own drop-off points.

Blue and green bins are collected every week. The black bin is collected every two weeks.

Bins must be at the curb by 6:30 on collection day and taken back in by 20:00 the same day.

If a bin has the wrong items in it, the crew leaves a tag on the lid and does not empty it. The resident fixes the bin and it is collected next time.

After three tags in twelve months, a household gets a letter and a visit from a recycling adviser. There is no fine for households.

Batteries and small electronics must never go in any bin; they can start fires in the trucks. They go to the drop-off box at the library or the recycling center.

The recycling center on Mill Road takes large items, paint and electronics free of charge for residents, with proof of address.

## For haulers

Haulers are the private companies under contract to collect Halbrook's bins.

A crew must inspect the top layer of each blue bin by opening the lid before tipping it. The contract calls this a "lid check".

If the visible contamination is more than about 10% of the bin, the crew tags it and does not tip it. Below that, the crew tips it.

Each tag is logged in the hauler's handheld app with the address, a photo and the reason code. Tags without a photo do not count toward the three-tag rule.

The sorting plant tests a sample of each truckload. A load above 15% contamination is rejected and charged to the hauler at the landfill rate.

The landfill rate is 92 euros per tonne. Accepted recycling costs the hauler nothing at the plant gate.

Hauler trucks must be weighed at the plant gate, both full and empty, on every trip.

A hauler that has more than 5% of its loads rejected in a quarter must submit a corrective plan within 30 days.

Haulers must report missed collections to Public Works the same day through the contract portal.

Crews must never compact a load that contains a visible lithium battery; they set the battery aside in the fireproof box on the truck.

## Why the rules changed

The sorting plant upgraded its optical sorters, which can now pick out plastic tubs as well as bottles.

Glass was removed from the blue bin because broken glass was ending up in the paper bales and lowering their sale price.

## Corrections

- The old leaflet said greasy pizza boxes go in the blue bin. That was wrong then and is wrong now: they go in the green bin.
- A draft of this summary said households could be fined 50 euros for a third tag. That was removed from the final ordinance. There is no household fine.
