# Ward 7B (Acute Medicine) — Local Induction Handbook for Newly Registered Nurses

Local protocol of a fictional teaching hospital, used for training only. Every statement here is the ward's own rule; where it differs from national guidance, the ward rule applies on 7B.

## The ward

Ward 7B has 28 beds in four 6-bed bays and four side rooms.

Side rooms are kept for patients in isolation. A patient with suspected infectious diarrhoea moves to a side room within 2 hours of the first loose stool.

Shifts run 07:30–20:00 and 19:30–08:00. The 30-minute overlap is for the bedside handover.

Handover is done at the bedside using the SBAR order: situation, background, assessment, recommendation.

Each registered nurse looks after one bay of 6 patients on a day shift and up to 8 patients on a night shift.

## Observations and escalation

Every patient has a full set of observations at least every 6 hours, and an early warning score is calculated each time.

Score 0–4: continue routine observations.

Score 5–6, or any single parameter scoring 3: tell the nurse in charge and the ward doctor within 30 minutes, and repeat observations every hour.

Score 7 or more: call the critical care outreach team on bleep 2222 at once, and repeat observations every 30 minutes.

A patient you are worried about can be escalated whatever the score. Worry is a valid reason on 7B and is written in the notes as "nurse concern".

Every escalation is written in the notes with the time, the person called, and the plan agreed.

## Medicines

Medicines are given on the 08:00, 12:00, 18:00 and 22:00 rounds.

Before giving any medicine, check the patient's identity against the wristband: full name, date of birth and hospital number.

Check the allergy band colour: a red wristband means a recorded allergy.

Controlled drugs are checked and signed for by two registered nurses, both present at the bedside when the dose is given.

The controlled drugs cupboard keys are held by the nurse in charge for the whole shift.

The controlled drug stock is counted at every shift handover by the outgoing and the incoming nurse in charge.

Intravenous antibiotics prescribed as time-critical must be given within 30 minutes of the prescribed time.

A missed or delayed time-critical dose is reported on the incident system the same shift.

Insulin is never drawn up with a standard syringe; only insulin syringes or pen devices are used.

A patient's own medicines brought from home are stored in the locked bedside locker, not in the ward stock.

## Falls and pressure care

Every patient has a falls risk assessment within 6 hours of arrival on the ward and after any fall.

A patient at high falls risk has a bed at the lowest height, a call bell within reach, and non-slip socks.

After a fall, the patient is not moved until checked by a nurse for injury. A suspected head injury needs a doctor review and neuro observations every 15 minutes for the first hour.

Every patient has a pressure ulcer risk assessment within 6 hours of arrival.

A patient at high pressure ulcer risk is repositioned at least every 2 hours, and each turn is recorded on the repositioning chart.

## Infection control

Hands are cleaned before and after every patient contact, using soap and water (not alcohol gel) after caring for a patient with diarrhoea.

Gloves and aprons are changed between patients and between dirty and clean tasks on the same patient.

Sharps go in the sharps bin at the point of use; a bin is closed and replaced when it reaches the fill line.

A needlestick injury is washed under running water, encouraged to bleed, and reported to occupational health within 1 hour.
