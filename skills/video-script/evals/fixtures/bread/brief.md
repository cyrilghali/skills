# Brief: u01-why-bread-rises — Why bread rises

- **Series goal:** home bakers understand what each step of a bread recipe does, so they can tell why a loaf failed.
- **Audience — home baker:** people who bake from recipes at home; no chemistry or biology. Already knows: how to follow a recipe.
- **Tier:** overview (1 of 2); stands alone: yes
- **The one idea:** bread rises because yeast makes gas and a stretchy web in the dough holds that gas in.
- **Length:** about 60 seconds
- **Prerequisites:** none

## Facts this unit must say

| id | fact | source |
|---|---|---|
| f01 | Yeast feeds on sugars in the flour and gives off carbon dioxide and ethanol. | source.md |
| f02 | Kneading wet flour builds gluten, a network of proteins that stretches and springs back. | source.md |
| f03 | The gluten network traps the gas in bubbles, so the dough swells. | source.md |
| f04 | In the oven, the gas expands and the ethanol turns to vapour, giving a last fast rise called oven spring. | source.md |
| f05 | Heat sets the crumb around the bubbles, so the loaf keeps its volume. | source.md |
