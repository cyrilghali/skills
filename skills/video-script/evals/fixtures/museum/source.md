# Our history

From the website of the Harbour Museum, Port Ellery.

The Harbour Museum began in 1911 as a single room above the Port Ellery customs house. Its founder, Agnes Rowe, was the daughter of a harbour pilot. She started collecting after the 1907 storm destroyed half the fishing fleet, because she feared the town's boats and tools would vanish with the men who used them.

Her first catalogue, written by hand in 1912, lists 214 objects: nets, lanterns, ship models, logbooks and a set of pilot's charts that had belonged to her father.

Local legend says the first object in the collection was the carved figurehead of the schooner Marianne, which Rowe is said to have pulled from the harbour herself after the storm. No record confirms this: the figurehead does not appear in the 1912 catalogue and is first recorded in 1923.

In 1934 the town council bought the old rope works on Quay Street and the collection moved there, where it still stands. The museum became a registered charity in 1952.

Today the museum holds about 9,000 objects. Rowe's handwritten catalogue is on display in the first gallery.
